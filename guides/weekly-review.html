Weekly habit review: what to look for

A good weekly review turns a week of small entries into one or two practical changes for the next week.

1. Check the overall completion rate

Look at the week as a whole before focusing on one difficult day. A broad view makes it easier to separate an isolated miss from a repeated pattern.

2. Compare current and best streaks

A streak is useful context, but it should not be the only number you inspect. Compare it with completion rates and your calendar to understand whether the routine is generally stable.

3. Identify weak habits

Sort your attention toward habits that repeatedly have low completion. There is little value in spending the whole review redesigning a habit that already works.

4. Read your notes

Notes provide context for exceptions. Look for repeated phrases such as tired, late, busy, forgot or no time. The goal is to convert repeated friction into a design change.

5. Compare the calendar

Calendar views are useful for seeing whether weak days cluster around particular dates or parts of your schedule. Even a simple visual scan can reveal patterns that are hard to notice from memory.

6. Change one thing

A review is more effective when it produces a small experiment. Change one habit target, one reminder time or one routine step, then track the result for another week.

7. Keep what already works

Do not redesign successful parts of the system just because another habit is struggling. Good tracking is partly about identifying stable behaviors and leaving them alone while you test improvements elsewhere.

A weekly review is successful when it produces a clear next experiment, not a long list of changes.
