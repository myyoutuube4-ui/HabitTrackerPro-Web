How to review 30 days of habit data

A simple monthly review process for using completion rates, calendar patterns and notes to improve your next month.

HabitTracker Pro guideUpdated October 1, 2026Practical framework

Start with the whole month

Look at your overall completion pattern before focusing on one difficult day. A monthly view helps you see whether the routine is broadly stable or whether the result depends on a few unusually strong days.

Find the most reliable habits

Identify habits that were completed consistently with little effort. These are useful anchors in your routine. You do not need to redesign a habit that is already working simply because another one is struggling.

Find repeated weak points

Next, look for habits with repeated misses or partial entries. A single miss may be random. A repeated pattern suggests something about the schedule, size of the habit, cue or environment needs attention.

Compare the calendar with your notes

Numbers show what happened; notes can explain context. If weak days cluster around particular dates, compare your notes with those dates. You may discover a recurring schedule conflict or another predictable source of friction.

Check whether the habit list changed

If you added, paused or renamed habits during the month, keep that context in mind when interpreting percentages. A changing denominator can make a month look different even when the routine itself did not change dramatically.

Choose one experiment for the next month

The purpose of the review is not to produce a perfect report. Pick one practical change: reduce a target, change a cue, move a reminder, remove a redundant habit or clarify the completion rule. Then observe the effect in the next review cycle.

Key takeaway: Thirty days of data becomes useful when it leads to one clear experiment for the next thirty days.
